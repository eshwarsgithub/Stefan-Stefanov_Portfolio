## 1. Executive Summary

Stefan Stefanov is a 37-year-old Bulgarian entrepreneur who, at the end of 2025, successfully exited his electric vehicle (EV) charging station maintenance business in Germany. Following the sale and payment of applicable German taxes, Stefan deposited **€700,000** into a Bulgarian bank account on January 1, 2026. This report presents a structured, rules-based investment portfolio designed to compound that capital over a 20–28 year horizon and support a financially independent retirement between 2052 and 2056.

The portfolio allocates €700,000 across **four asset classes** and **thirteen UCITS-compliant instruments**, all denominated in euros. Every allocation decision is anchored in a written Investment Policy Statement (IPS) and explicitly designed to counter five behavioral biases that consistently derail long-term individual investors.

### 2.2 Required Return — Derivation

Stefan's 6.0–6.5% annual target return is not arbitrary. It is derived from two components:

- **Inflation protection:** The European Central Bank (ECB) targets 2.0% long-term inflation. A portfolio growing below inflation loses real purchasing power — Stefan's retirement standard of living falls in real terms even if the nominal portfolio number grows.
- **Real growth requirement:** To fund 26+ years of retirement withdrawals, sustain the annual Alps vacation (~€10,000 per year), and build a meaningful legacy, the portfolio must deliver a real return of 4.0–4.5% above inflation annually.

**Required return = 2.0% (inflation) + 4.0–4.5% (real growth) = 6.0–6.5% p.a.**

This target is achievable with a 55% equity allocation over a 20–28 year horizon, consistent with historical MSCI World annualised returns of 8–10% in EUR terms (1970–2025, MSCI data).

### 2.3 Investment Policy Statement — Core Rules

The IPS transforms Stefan's values and risk tolerance into binding rules that govern every investment decision:

1. **Drawdown limit:** Portfolio drawdown must not exceed −22% at any point. If breached, equities are reduced toward the glide path floor.
2. **Cost ceiling:** Blended TER must not exceed 0.30% per annum. No actively managed funds.
3. **Rebalancing trigger:** If any asset class drifts more than ±5% from its target weight, rebalance within 30 days regardless of market conditions.
4. **No individual stock positions:** All equity exposure through diversified index ETFs only. No single company represents more than its index weight.
5. **Currency rule:** All instruments denominated in EUR. No unhedged USD, GBP, or other currency exposure.
6. **Liquidity reserve:** A minimum 3% cash equivalent (MMF) must be maintained at all times for behavioral stability and lifestyle costs.

### 2.4 Investment Goals

**Primary — Retirement Security (2052–2056):**
Stefan is 37 years old. At a 6.5% annual compound return, €700,000 grows to approximately **€3.75 million by 2052** (base scenario, 26 years). This sum, drawn at 3.5% per year (a sustainable withdrawal rate), generates €131,000 annually — well above any lifestyle maintenance requirement. The portfolio is designed to reach this milestone without requiring any active management decisions from Stefan.

**Secondary — Lifestyle Maintenance:**
The 3% cash/MMF allocation (€21,000) is specifically earmarked for Stefan's annual lifestyle costs, including the Alps vacation (~€10,000). This pocket ensures he never needs to liquidate equity positions at an unfavorable moment to fund routine expenses.

**Tertiary — Legacy and Values:**
Stefan's professional background in clean energy infrastructure reflects a personal alignment with the energy transition. The portfolio incorporates this through the INFR and SAWD positions — not out of sentiment, but because Stefan's domain expertise allows him to identify the structural investment case more confidently than an average investor.

## 3. Macroeconomic Analysis — 2026 Global Environment

A portfolio constructed without reference to the current economic environment is a portfolio built on assumptions. This section analyses the macro conditions that directly shaped each allocation decision.

### 3.1 Global Growth Outlook

According to the IMF World Economic Outlook (April 2026), global GDP is projected to grow at **3.1%** in 2026. Growth is notably uneven:

- **United States: 2.2%** — resilient consumer spending, AI-driven capital investment, but elevated public debt ($36 trillion) and fiscal pressure.
- **Eurozone: 0.9%** — sluggish but recovering; ECB rate cuts are stimulating credit demand; Germany's industrial base is beginning to stabilise.
- **Emerging Markets: 4.1%** — led by India (6.8%) and Southeast Asia; supply-chain diversification away from China is creating new industrial corridors.
- **China: 4.5%** — below historical trend; structural challenges in real estate and demographics weigh on long-term potential.

This environment supports a **globally diversified equity approach** over country or region concentration. A portfolio focused solely on Eurozone stocks (0.9% growth) or US stocks (elevated valuations) would sacrifice either growth or safety. Stefan's portfolio captures all three growth regions through IWDA, EXW1, and EIMI.

### 3.2 ECB Monetary Policy — The Rate Cutting Cycle

Perhaps the most important macro development for this portfolio is the ECB's confirmed monetary policy pivot. After raising rates aggressively from 0% to 4.0% between 2022 and 2023 to combat post-pandemic inflation, the ECB began cutting in mid-2024. By May 2026, the deposit rate stands at **2.50%**, with further cuts expected toward 2.0% by end-2026 (ECB forward guidance).

The implications for this portfolio are direct and significant:

- **Bond prices rise as rates fall.** Stefan's fixed income allocation (IEGY, IEBC) benefits from capital appreciation as yields decline — a tactical advantage available specifically in 2026 that would not exist in a flat or rising rate environment.
- **Lower rates reduce the discount rate on equities**, supporting valuations across IWDA and EXW1.
- **The BULGAR eurobond** (3.125% coupon) was issued at a time when ECB rates were higher; as rates fall, its market price appreciates above face value.

The January 2026 deployment date was therefore not arbitrary — it coincides with the peak benefit window of the ECB cutting cycle.

### 3.3 Inflation — Disinflation Confirmed

Eurozone consumer price inflation peaked at **10.6% in October 2022**, driven by the energy shock following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. By May 2026, CPI has moderated to **2.2%**, very close to the ECB's 2.0% target. Disinflation has been orderly and sustained.

This matters for two reasons:

1. **Equities become more attractive** when inflation falls. High inflation imposes a discount on future earnings; at 2.2%, the headwind is gone.
2. **Residual inflation risk is not zero.** Structural pressures — energy transition infrastructure costs, housing supply constraints, European defence ramp-up — mean inflation could re-accelerate above 3% if supply-side shocks repeat. The 7% real assets allocation (INFR, IWDP) specifically addresses this tail risk.

### 3.4 Geopolitical Context

Three geopolitical forces shaped allocation decisions:

**Russia-Ukraine conflict:** The ongoing war has permanently accelerated Europe's clean energy transition. The EU's REPowerEU plan and national energy independence programs have committed over **€800 billion** to energy infrastructure between 2022 and 2030. Stefan's EV charging background positions him to recognise this as a structural investment tailwind — INFR holds utilities and infrastructure companies that directly benefit from this capital deployment.

**US-China technology decoupling:** The accelerating separation of US and Chinese technology supply chains is creating new winners in emerging markets — particularly India, Vietnam, and Mexico, all of which feature in EIMI. The 8% emerging market allocation is not a speculative bet on China; it is a diversified play on the supply-chain diversification that decoupling drives.

**Middle East tension:** Ongoing instability in the Middle East creates a residual tail risk for oil prices. A sudden spike would hurt European consumer spending and compress equity returns. The infrastructure allocation (INFR) provides partial offset — energy infrastructure revenues tend to be positively correlated with commodity price environments.

### 3.5 Bulgaria's Eurozone Accession — 2026

For a Bulgarian investor, this is the most locally significant development in a generation. Bulgaria joined the Eurozone in 2026, converting the Bulgarian lev (BGN) to the euro at the fixed rate established by the Currency Board since 1997.

The practical implications for Stefan's portfolio:

- **Currency risk is permanently eliminated.** Every instrument in this portfolio is EUR-denominated. Stefan's income (from portfolio returns) and expenses (in Bulgaria, increasingly EUR-priced) are now in the same currency. No exchange rate drag erodes his returns.
- **ECB monetary framework access** improves the stability of Bulgarian financial markets and reduces the country risk premium embedded in BULGAR's yield.
- **The Bulgaria Republic Eurobond (3.125%, 2034)** becomes strategically more attractive post-accession — it now benefits from ECB backing and reduced sovereign risk premium.

## 4. Asset Allocation — Strategic Rationale

The portfolio is structured around four asset classes. The weights — 55% equities, 35% fixed income, 7% real assets, 3% cash — were determined by the intersection of Stefan's financial goals, risk tolerance, and the 2026 macroeconomic environment.

### 4.1 Equities — 55% (€385,000)

At 37 years old with a 20–28 year horizon, Stefan has the single most valuable asset in investing: **time**. The equity risk premium — the excess return that equities have historically delivered above risk-free assets — requires time to realise. Over any 20-year rolling window since 1970, the MSCI World has never delivered a negative annualised return in EUR terms (MSCI data).

The 55% ceiling (not higher) is set by the IPS's −22% maximum drawdown constraint. A simulated 55/45 equity/bond portfolio has historically experienced a maximum drawdown of approximately −21% (2000–2025 data), consistent with Stefan's tolerance.

The 55% is diversified across five ETFs representing different market segments: global developed markets (IWDA, 25%), Eurozone (EXW1, 10%), emerging markets (EIMI, 8%), global ESG-screened (SAWD, 7%), and global information technology (XDWT, 5%).

### 4.2 Fixed Income — 35% (€245,000)

Kahneman and Tversky's Prospect Theory (1979) demonstrates that investors feel losses approximately twice as intensely as equivalent gains. A portfolio with no fixed income experiences greater volatility, which increases the probability of panic selling at market lows. The 35% bond allocation is therefore not primarily a return generator — it is **behavioral architecture** that makes Stefan's target return achievable because it keeps him invested during drawdowns.

The fixed income sleeve is deliberately structured across five sub-buckets with different risk/return profiles: EUR government bonds for safety (IEGY, 12%), investment-grade corporate bonds for yield pick-up (IEBC, 10%), high yield for additional income capped at 5% (IHYG, 5%), the Bulgaria sovereign bond for strategic positioning (BULGAR, 4%), and a short-duration buffer to manage interest rate risk (CSH2, 4%).

### 4.3 Real Assets — 7% (€49,000)

While inflation has fallen to 2.2%, structural pressures from the energy transition, housing shortages, and defence spending mean that above-target inflation is a plausible scenario over a 26-year horizon. Infrastructure assets (INFR, 4%) generate revenues that are explicitly inflation-linked through regulated utility contracts. Real estate investment trusts (IWDP, 3%) provide property market exposure — historically one of the strongest long-run inflation hedges — without the illiquidity of direct property ownership.

### 4.4 Cash — 3% (€21,000)

The MMF allocation is not idle capital. It is a **pre-committed behavioral firewall**. Its specific purposes are: (a) cover Stefan's annual Alps vacation and lifestyle expenses for approximately two years without touching the investment portfolio; (b) prevent forced equity selling at market lows; and (c) provide optionality to increase equity exposure during significant market corrections. The Dr. Ivanchev framework of pre-agreed rules — rather than in-the-moment decisions — is the foundation of this pocket.

## 6. Risk Management

### 6.1 Portfolio Volatility and Drawdown

The 55/35/7/3 allocation produces an estimated portfolio volatility of 9–11% per annum and a maximum historical drawdown of approximately −22%, consistent with the IPS limit. This was validated against four historical stress scenarios:

| Crisis | Market Impact | Estimated Portfolio Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Global Financial Crisis (2008–2009) | Equity −54% | Portfolio ~−22% to −26% |
| COVID-19 (Feb–Mar 2020) | Equity −34% | Portfolio ~−15% to −18% |
| Rate Shock (2022) | Bonds −14%, Equity −18% | Portfolio ~−12% to −14% |
| Geopolitical shock (2026 scenario) | Equity −18%, Bonds −5% | Portfolio ~−10% to −12% |

In each scenario, the fixed income and real assets allocations provide meaningful portfolio protection.

### 6.2 Behavioral Finance Safeguards

Given that Dr. Boyan Ivanchev's research identifies behavioral biases as the primary cause of individual investor underperformance, the portfolio is explicitly designed to address five documented risks:

- **Loss aversion:** The 35% bond allocation keeps portfolio drawdowns within Stefan's psychological tolerance, reducing the probability of panic selling.
- **Recency bias:** Fixed weights and rebalancing rules prevent the temptation to chase recent winners (e.g., increasing tech exposure after a strong run).
- **Overconfidence:** No individual stock positions. All equity exposure is passive and index-based, eliminating the illusion that any stock can be reliably predicted to outperform.
- **Home bias:** Bulgarian equities constitute less than 4% of the portfolio (via BULGAR), preventing over-concentration in a small, illiquid market.
- **Mental accounting:** The MMF reserve is explicitly designated for lifestyle expenses, preventing Stefan from mentally treating it as "extra money" to increase equity bets during bull markets.

## 7. Implementation

### 7.1 Entry Strategy — Dollar-Cost Averaging

Stefan does not invest all €700,000 on January 1, 2026. Market timing is the most reliably unsuccessful strategy in investing (Vanguard Research, 2012). Instead, the capital is deployed across three tranches over six months:

- **January 2026 (Tranche 1 — 50%, €350,000):** Full fixed income and cash allocations, plus 60% of the equity target. This immediately establishes the defensive structure.
- **March 2026 (Tranche 2 — 30%, €210,000):** Remaining equity exposure deployed. Captures any market correction between January and March.
- **June 2026 (Tranche 3 — 20%, €140,000):** Real assets (INFR, IWDP) and top-up to full target weights.

### 7.2 Rebalancing Protocol

The portfolio is reviewed once per year (January) and rebalanced to target weights. Additionally, any asset class that drifts more than ±5% from its target triggers an immediate rebalance — independent of the annual schedule. This rule exists to prevent gradual equity creep (where rising equity markets quietly increase portfolio risk beyond the IPS limit) or under-performance drift (where falling bonds reduce the behavioral anchor below its minimum effective level).

### 7.3 Tax Efficiency

Bulgaria's 10% flat capital gains tax rate is one of the lowest in the European Union. All ETFs in this portfolio are domiciled in Ireland or Luxembourg, where dividends are subject to reduced withholding tax rates under double taxation treaties with Bulgaria. Gains are deferred until realisation — the long-term holding strategy maximises tax deferral, meaning Stefan compounds on the full pre-tax capital for decades before any tax event occurs.

## 8. Conclusion

Stefan Stefanov's €700,000 represents the financial reward of over a decade of entrepreneurial work in Germany's clean energy sector. This portfolio is engineered to be the structured, disciplined counterpart to that event-driven liquidity.

Three principles define the investment thesis:

**Disciplined diversification:** Thirteen UCITS instruments, zero individual stock positions, four asset classes, and every major global equity market represented. No single decision, company, or sector can meaningfully impair the total portfolio.

**Behavioral architecture:** The IPS, the fixed-weight rebalancing rules, the dedicated MMF lifestyle reserve, and the prohibition on stock picking are not restrictions — they are Stefan's protection against himself during the inevitable market downturns of the next 26 years. The investor who stays invested, wins.

**Macro-informed, long-term positioning:** The 2026 ECB cutting cycle, confirmed Eurozone disinflation, and Bulgaria's Eurozone accession collectively create a deployment environment where fixed income benefits from capital appreciation, equity valuations are uncompressed by inflation, and currency risk for a Bulgarian investor is permanently eliminated. Stefan's own expertise in EV charging infrastructure connects him intuitively to the energy transition tailwind that runs through INFR and SAWD.

At a 6.5% base return over 26 years, €700,000 grows to approximately **€3.75 million by 2052** — a retirement asset base that, drawn at a 3.5% withdrawal rate, generates over €130,000 annually. Stefan does not need to pick the right stock, time the market, or monitor a Bloomberg terminal. He needs to commit to the plan, rebalance once a year, and let compounding do the work.
